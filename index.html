🌈 How is a rainbow made?

Does one raindrop make the whole rainbow? Or is it made by lots of raindrops working together? Let's play with the light and find out.

1 Follow light through ONE raindrop

Sunlight looks white, but it's secretly made of all the colors. When a ray hits a raindrop it bends going in (①), bounces off the back wall (②), and bends again coming out (③). It comes out heading mostly back toward the sun.

Tip: click or drag on the drop to move the ray. The graph shows the leaving angle for every place a ray can hit.

2 One drop, many directions, ONE color for you

Every color leaves the drop at a slightly different angle: red a little wider, violet a little narrower. So a single drop sends all the colors out, but in different directions. Where you stand decides which one reaches your eye. Move the eye around the drop!

3 Now add lots of drops

You're standing with the sun behind you, looking at falling rain. Each drop sends you just one color, and only if it sits at exactly the right angle from the point your shadow's head points to. Add drops and watch.

Click or drag in the sky to move the white-ringed drop and see what it sends you.

Same thing from the side

A slice of sky straight in front of you. Drops inside the colored wedge send color to your eye; drops outside it send you nothing.

4 So why not lots of little rainbows?

  • One drop does NOT make a whole rainbow. It sends all its colors out, but each color goes a different way. You only catch one color from it.
  • The color you catch depends on the angle: about 42° from your shadow's direction is red, about 40° is violet, and the colors fall in between.
  • All drops at the same angle send you the same color, so they line up on a circle around the point your shadow points to. Red ring on the outside, violet inside, the rest in between. That's the rainbow.
  • A drop in front of or behind another one at the same angle doesn't make a second rainbow. It adds to the same ring, and the rainbow gets brighter.
  • That's why a rainbow isn't a thing in one place. It's a pattern of angles. You can never walk up to it, it moves when you move, and your friend standing next to you is looking at a slightly different rainbow made by different drops.
  • Try the sun-height slider: when the sun is higher than about 42°, the ring is below the horizon and you can't see it from the ground.
  • Bonus: a faint second rainbow appears when light bounces twice inside a drop. It comes out at about 51°, with the colors upside down.
